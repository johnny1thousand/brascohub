How it works

Four steps, and the box is catalogued

The whole point is that you keep your hands on the comics instead of a keyboard.

1

Photograph the cover

In the browser, on the phone already in your pocket. Nothing to install, nothing to plug in.

2

It reads the cover

Character, book name, issue number, publisher, year — and a line on why the issue matters, like a first appearance or a famous run. It shows you what it found before anything is saved, so a wrong guess is one tap to fix.

3

It straightens the shot

The photo is cropped to the edges of the book and de-skewed, so a picture taken at arm's length on a kitchen table still looks square on the shelf.

4

It files itself

Grouped by character and by book, with the gaps in a run called out — #300–#303, missing #302. Mark the ones that matter as grails or favorites.

Why not a spreadsheet

Because a spreadsheet never shows you the cover

Gaps in a run, flagged

It knows you have #300, #301 and #303, and tells you which issue you're hunting.

Grails and favorites

Two taps to mark the books you'd save from a fire. They sort to the top of the shelf.

Search that finds it

Character, title, issue, publisher, condition, your own notes — one box, everything in it.

Works with no signal

Catalogue a box in a basement or at a show. Edits queue up on the phone and sync when you're back.

Condition and value

Grade, what it's worth, what you paid, when you got it — visible only to you.

Your data, exportable

The whole collection downloads as a CSV whenever you want it. No lock-in, no hostage-taking.
